WHY THESE NEXT
==============
PDFs are read by the customer's own staff and occasionally forwarded. These
emails go OUTBOUND by default - to subcontractors, to a customer's design team,
to the people a customer is chasing for money. A subcontractor receiving a
training-renewal demand signed "Rock Roofing Ltd" from a different company is
worse than an unbranded PDF.

24 strings across seven files, all now companyName().

THE CHASE TEMPLATE PLACEHOLDER - READ THIS ONE
===============================================
The seeded chase letters signed off with the merge token

    [Rock Roofing QS Name]

That is a placeholder, not prose, and it sits alongside [Customer QS Name] -
so the pair reads "their QS" and "our QS". It is now [Our QS Name] in the
seeded examples.

BUT: templates a customer has already EDITED are saved in their own database
and still contain the old token. So pages/outstanding-invoices.js now
substitutes BOTH. Removing the old one would have silently stopped substituting
in exactly the templates someone had bothered to customise - they would have
sent with a literal "[Rock Roofing QS Name]" in the signature.

Rock's saved templates keep working untouched. New tenants get the neutral one.

The signature block at the foot of a chase email also hard-coded "Rock
Roofing". senderSignature() and buildBody() are module-scope helpers, so the
brand is threaded through as a PARAMETER rather than read from a hook. Uglier,
and much harder to get wrong: a missed argument shows as a blank line, not as
one customer's name on another's letter.
