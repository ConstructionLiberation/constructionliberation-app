THE POINT OF THE SECTION IS THE RECONCILIATION
===============================================
Bookkeeping exists to get every cost, wage and sales invoice out of Xero and
onto a project. Its value is the WORKING THROUGH: an unassigned line sits in
the list until somebody gives it a project, and then it clears. Seeding
everything as already-assigned would show a set of empty tabs and prove
nothing.

So April through August are fully assigned and balance, and SEPTEMBER has a
pile waiting:

    26 untagged bills      16 project costs to allocate, 10 genuine overheads
    11 untagged wages      direct wages, week 36-39, no project
     7 unassigned sales    invoices with no tracking category
     9 in-query costs      parked, deliberately excluded from project spend

Assign one and watch the line clear - that is the demo.

The mix of project costs and real overheads in the untagged bills is
deliberate. If everything needed a project, categorising would be mechanical;
the judgement is in telling the two apart, which is what the screen is for.


WHAT ELSE WENT IN
=================
CHART OF ACCOUNTS and ACCOUNT CATEGORISATION. 16 codes with names and
categories - sales, materials, labour, overheads. Without these every code
reads as "uncategorised" and the app raises a banner about it, so the
categorisation feature could not be shown working.

THE IN QUERY BUCKET. Matched on the tracking option NAME, not a flag -
pages/api/bookkeeping.js compares the project name to "inquery" with spaces
stripped. It is now a real row in the project list called "In Query" with nine
costs against it: a queried price, a wrong site, a suspected duplicate. Those
are deliberately excluded from project spend until somebody decides where they
belong.


ONE THING WORTH RECORDING
==========================
The In Query cost lines were being referenced in the dashboard cache before
they were declared. JavaScript hoists the declaration but not the value, so it
would have been undefined at that point - no compile error, and an empty
In Query tab with nothing to explain it. Caught by running the generator
rather than by the compiler.
