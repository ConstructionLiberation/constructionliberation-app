THE PATTERN IN MY MISTAKES
===========================
I have been generating plausible figures rather than data that makes the app's
flags, mismatches and drill-downs do anything. A demo is not about the numbers
being right - it is about the SCREENS DOING SOMETHING when you click them. All
four fixes below are that.


1. NO PROJECT WITH AN EMPTY ROW
================================
J106 and J117 showed zero invoiced and zero spend. Both were jobs started this
month, so their first application falls after the valuation date. Technically
correct, reads as missing data.

Minimum project age is now three months. Measured: zero empty projects.


2. CERTIFIED vs INVOICED - THE FLAG NOW MEANS SOMETHING
========================================================
Certified always differed from invoiced by a random 0-12%, so EVERY row
carried the "check FA" warning and the flag told you nothing.

Now three in four reconcile exactly and the rest are genuinely out. Measured:
20 of 30 match.

One subtlety worth knowing: it is matched against the INVOICED total, not the
applied-for total, because credit notes reduce what was invoiced. Comparing to
applied-for meant every project with a credit note showed as unreconciled.


3. THE 612 COLUMNS
===================
612 Deducted and 612 Released were empty. 612 is the retention control
account, and the tracker exists to compare what the customer deducted against
what we say is owed.

Now populated: most reconcile, a quarter are out by a clear margin, completed
projects have a first release paid, and each has ret612Lines so the cell
drills down to the movements behind it.

The mismatch is deliberately 12-38% out rather than a couple of per cent - a
small difference rounds to the same number often enough that the mismatch
count drifted to 2 in 30 instead of the intended 1 in 4.

ALSO FIXED: retention612Released was being set twice in the same object, the
real figure first and a placeholder 0 second. A later key wins in a JavaScript
object literal, so the column was being zeroed after being filled. esbuild's
duplicate-key warning caught it - worth noting because nothing else would
have.


4. WIP WITH SOMETHING TO OPEN
==============================
One post-valuation cost line per project gives a WIP figure and nothing to
examine. The first two projects now have FIVE and TEN lines respectively -
different suppliers, account codes and dates inside the window - so the
drill-down is worth opening. Both are forced live, since a completed project
has no work in progress by definition.

16 of 30 projects have August WIP.
