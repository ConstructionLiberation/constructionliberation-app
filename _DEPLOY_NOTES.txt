FORECAST SALES - new card, under Total sales
  Source    the Forecast P&L (12-month forecast).
  Card      the total revenue in the FORECAST months still to come this
            financial year - the "£877,055 forecast" on the Forecast P&L's
            Revenue box. "Rest of FY2026", with "Sep 26 - Nov 26, 3 forecast
            months" underneath.
  Graph     each forecast month's revenue, in its own column.
  Actual and manual months are not forecast, so they are not counted.

FORECAST GROSS MARGIN
  Source    the Forecast P&L, NOT the balance sheet: its Gross profit
            percentage - the whole financial year, actual months + forecast
            months + the WIP and year-end WIP adjustments. 26.6% on the
            current figures.
  Card      the figure only. No graph, and no row in the trend table.

Both are the CURRENT financial year's forecast whatever From/To is set to - a
forecast is about the year still running.


HOW THE FIGURES ARE GUARANTEED TO MATCH THE FORECAST P&L PAGE
The Forecast P&L is built in the browser from three Business Financials views.
The scorecard now builds it on the server from the SAME three views - by
calling the Business Financials route's own body, not a copy - and the SAME
model with the SAME saved settings (manual months, WIP lock, WIP include,
year-end WIP). One function, lib/forecastMonths.js forecastFromViews, holds the
settings-to-model step.

Management users get the two numbers. They do not get access to the
admin-only Business Financials reports - the admin check is skipped only for
this internal call, which the scorecard makes after its own management check.

Business Financials itself is unchanged: the route's auth check was moved
into a two-line wrapper so the body could be called; every view behaves as
before.

Cost: the three views are read on each scorecard load. Fine at this size;
if the page feels slow, that is where to look.


DECEMBER-ONLY, BY DESIGN
The Forecast P&L assumes a December year. The forecast cards therefore show
only when fyStartMonth is 12; any other year start gets a notice rather than
a December forecast set against another year's actuals.


MONTH LABELS
Column and axis labels are now built from the month text itself. Before, the
page turned '2025-12' into a date at midnight UTC, which anywhere behind UTC is
still 30 November. Not the cause of what you saw from NZ, but it would have
been for anyone viewing from the Americas.
