WHAT DELIBERATELY DID NOT CHANGE, AND WHY IT MATTERS
================================================================================
1. The SPREADSHEET COLUMN at lines 463-464:

       pick(r, 'VAT Type', 'vatRateLabel')

   That is the literal header in the Xero CSV being imported. Renaming it
   would break the import for every existing file, and it is not a label -
   nobody sees it.

2. The DROPDOWN VALUES: '20%', '5%', '0% reverse charge', 'No VAT', 'Mixed'.
   These are STORED on the record as vatRateLabel and parsed back into a rate
   by parseVatRate(). Changing 'No VAT' to 'No GST' would change stored data
   and stop the parser recognising its own values. They are data wearing the
   appearance of labels, which is exactly the trap term() is easy to fall
   into.

   It also means an NZ tenant sees a heading that says GST above a dropdown
   offering 20% and 5% - UK rates. That is honest rather than tidy: the rates
   come from their Xero, and pretending otherwise would be worse. Making the
   list locale-aware is a real change, not a relabel.

3. Nothing COMPUTES from term('vat'). The word maps; the tax does not.
   lib/terms.js marks this entry statutory with that note attached.

PAYMENT CLAIM - I HAVE NOT DONE THIS AND IT NEEDS YOUR DECISION
================================================================================
term('application') maps to "payment claim" for NZ, and it is the change WPC
would notice most. But the word appears in three quite different places:

  ON SCREEN    the Commercial nav item, page headings, table labels
  IN THE PDF   the document title on what you send a client
  IN EMAILS    the subject line and body of the covering email

The screen is a relabel and safe. THE PDF AND THE EMAIL ARE NOT. Under the
NZ Construction Contracts Act a payment claim has prescribed content - it
must state that it is made under the Act, and for residential work it must
carry the prescribed outline of process. A document headed "Payment Claim"
that does not do those things is worse than one headed "Application for
Payment", because it looks like it is claiming a statutory status it has not
earned. That is precisely what the statutory flag in lib/terms.js is warning
about.

My recommendation: relabel the SCREEN only, and leave the PDF and email in
British wording until someone who knows the Act has looked at the document
itself. Tell WPC that, in those words - it reads as competence rather than a
gap.
