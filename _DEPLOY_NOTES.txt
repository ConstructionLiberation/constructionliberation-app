pkg928 - Stop the daily health alert crying wolf
=================================================

REPLACE (1 file, existing - no new files to create):

  lib/cronHealth.js


WHAT WAS WRONG
--------------
Two jobs were reported OVERDUE every morning while working perfectly.

The expected interval is derived from the cron expression, which assumes a
job runs whenever it is invoked. These two do not - they are invoked daily
and then decide it is not their day:

  forms-weekly-notify      Mondays only, inside the hs-expiry-email dispatcher
  design-rfi-outstanding   every third WORKING day

pkg906 stopped a skipped run overwriting the heartbeat of a real one, which
was right - that had hidden a genuinely broken job for weeks. The side
effect is that these jobs legitimately show an age far longer than their
schedule implies, so they read as dead.

The timings in Friday's email confirm it exactly: 96.5h back from Friday
08:30 is Monday 08:00, and design-rfi-outstanding was due to run at 09:00
that same morning - half an hour AFTER the alert said it was overdue.


WHAT CHANGED
------------
A declared cadence per job, overriding anything derived from the schedule.
Declared rather than derived because the cadence lives in an `if` inside
each job and no cron expression can describe it.

  forms-weekly-notify      7 days
  design-rfi-outstanding   5 days

Five, not three: the worst case is a Friday send, where Mon, Tue, Wed is the
third working day - five calendar days later.

Tested both ways. At the ages from Friday's email both read healthy; at
175h and 145h - a genuinely missed slot - both still fire.


WHY THIS MATTERS MORE THAN IT LOOKS
------------------------------------
Two false alarms a day is how you stop opening the email. On 17 September
that same email also carried crm-daily-activities failing for real, sitting
underneath the two that were wrong.


AFTER DEPLOYING - CHECK
-----------------------
1. Tomorrow at 08:30 there should be NO health alert at all.
2. /api/admin/cron-health should show both jobs as ok rather than OVERDUE.
3. If an alert does arrive, read it - it now means something.
