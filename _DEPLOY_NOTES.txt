WHY THE LAST THREE PACKAGES DID NOT FIX THIS
=============================================
I was correcting field names while the underlying model was wrong. Costs were
generated scattered at random across each project's life; invoices were
generated from the applications. TWO UNRELATED TIMELINES.

At any given valuation date the two did not correspond. A project whose first
application fell after the valuation date had all of its costs counted and
none of its revenue - hence Invoiced 0.00 against six figures of spend, and a
portfolio margin of -16.9% on a dataset built to average 25%.

No amount of renaming fields was going to fix that, and I should have seen it
two packages ago rather than treating each symptom as a separate bug.

THE FIX: costs and invoices are now generated in the SAME LOOP, period by
period. Each month the project earns revenue and spends (1 - margin) of it. So
at any valuation date, costs-to-date over invoiced-to-date gives the margin BY
CONSTRUCTION rather than by coincidence.

Measured by simulating the page's own calculation at the August valuation
date:
    portfolio margin        25.1%
    per-project range       6% to 38%
    projects with zero      2 (brand-new jobs, and their costs are zero too,
      invoiced                so no negative margin)


RETENTION: THE MERGE KEY IS xeroId
===================================
The tracker merges saved entries onto project rows with
`if (e.xeroId && xeroByXid.has(e.xeroId))`. My entries had no xeroId, so they
could never attach - they appeared as separate tracker-only rows, giving 60
rows for 30 projects and leaving the project rows with no release dates.

I had matched on projectName in pkg945, which was a guess. It is xeroId.
Now set on every entry.


OVERDUE INVOICES: A QUARTER OF THE LIST
========================================
"Most are overdue" was correct and my measurement was answering the wrong
question - I measured overdue as a share of ALL invoices, most of which are
paid and never appear. The Outstanding Invoices page lists only UNPAID ones,
so that is the denominator that matters.

Now: anything not yet due is outstanding (normal), and only about one in six
older invoices is still unpaid. Measured: 41 invoices on the list, 11 overdue
= 27% of the list.


WIP
===
Costs now include a deliberate post-valuation spend in the 29th-31st window of
each month, sized at 5-22% of that period's cost. 13 of 30 projects have WIP
in the August window.
