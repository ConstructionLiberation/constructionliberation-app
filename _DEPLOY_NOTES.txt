ONE EMPTY ARRAY, FOUR EMPTY SCREENS
================================================================================
The seeder wrote `history: []` on every deal.

lib/crmValueChanges.js does not STORE value-change records - it DERIVES them
from each deal's history, on read, so they cannot drift from the deals they
came from. Its own comment says so. With an empty history there is nothing to
derive, which is why the Sales Dashboard was blank and three scorecard
metrics - total value of work priced, value priced to existing customers, and
projects priced over 200k - were empty for every estimator.

Not four missing datasets. One.


WHAT IS GENERATED NOW
================================================================================
A history walked forward from the day the deal was created to the stage it
sits in today: an opening price, a stage entry for each stage it passed
through, and a repricing on roughly a third of those steps. 190 deals produce
about 570 value-change records and 39 priced stage entries.

Two details that had to be right rather than plausible:

STAGE LABELS, NOT IDS. deriveValueChanges() matches PRICED_STAGES against the
LABEL written into the history - 'MC Unsecured' counts, 'MC Unsecured Not
Priced' deliberately does not. The seeder had only the ids, so there is now a
STAGE_LABEL map that mirrors STAGES in pages/crm.js exactly. An id in the
history would have derived nothing and looked identical to the bug being
fixed.

THE NUMBERS ARE GIVEN OUTRIGHT. deriveValueChanges() reads h.oldValue and
h.newValue where they exist and falls back to parsing them out of the text -
and parseMoneyPair() looks for a POUND SIGN. On a New Zealand tenant the text
path can never match, so relying on it would have worked on Rock and silently
produced nothing on demo.


A FAULT I FOUND BY RUNNING IT, NOT READING IT
================================================================================
The first version drifted: a deal card reading $250,000 with a history whose
last entry said 185,500. The arithmetic was fine and nobody would have
queried the scorecard, but anyone opening a deal sees the two disagree.

The walk now lands exactly on the deal's value. Checked across all 190:
zero deals whose history ends on a different figure.
