THE ONE PLACE THE WORDING LIVES
================================================================================
describeApplication() in lib/applications.js already produced the title used
by the PDF, the email subject and the filename - one rule, one place, which
is why this was a small job rather than a sweep.

It cannot call term() itself: lib/applications.js is imported by client
pages, so importing lib/locale.js would drag AsyncLocalStorage into the
browser bundle. The term is threaded in, as with resolveMergeFields in 957.

Two helpers now live beside it, because English does not cooperate:

  claimTitle()        'application for payment' -> 'Application for Payment'
                      A blunt capitalise-every-word gives "For".
  claimTitlePlural()  'Applications for Payment' moves the s to the FIRST
                      word; 'Payment Claims' to the last. The joining word is
                      the tell. Checked against all four locales.

WHAT THIS STILL NEEDS BEFORE A REAL NZ CUSTOMER SENDS ONE
================================================================================
The word is right. The DOCUMENT is not yet verified. A payment claim under
the Construction Contracts Act has prescribed content - it must state that it
is made under the Act, and for residential work it must be accompanied by the
prescribed outline of process.

None of that is in the PDF, and nothing here added it. For a demo that is
fine and you have decided so deliberately. Before an NZ customer sends a real
one it needs someone who knows the Act to read the document. That belongs on
the onboarding checklist, not in the code.


WHAT I HAVE DELIBERATELY NOT RENAMED
================================================================================
valuation -> progress claim. It is cosmetic in the glossary, but "valuation
date" is your internal cut-off date and runs through dozens of API routes and
stored fields. "Progress claim date" sitting next to "Payment Claim" would
confuse rather than localise.

RAMS -> SWMS. The glossary's own note says an AU SWMS has prescribed content
and a relabelled UK RAMS is not a compliant one. Payment claim is a document
you can fix before anyone pays; a RAMS is a safety document a worker signs
onto, and calling it a SWMS invites "so you are SWMS-compliant?" - to which
the answer is no. Leaving it reads as a UK product being straight.
